## 1. Executive recommendation

Use a **staged two-loop business model**:

| Loop | First test | First possible economics | GCL value capture | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Agency Builder | Unpaid shadow stewardship qualification | Equal units from a prospectively budgeted, company-funded, maximum-liability-capped pool | Verified nonparticipant end-customer margin—or another counsel-approved participant-independent source—may bound company affordability only | **Shadow now; money only after causal proof** |
| Professional Operator | Concierge B2B operator professional-development mentoring | Lawful payee’s net service proceeds under the actual contract minus a transparent fee | Transaction/platform fee | **First direct-value research candidate** |

Do not build a broad marketplace, percentage-based Agency formula, multi-tier plan, affiliate system, or generalized score engine before these two hypotheses are validated.

## 2. Economic design constraints

Every option must satisfy all of the following:

- no recruiting, signup, downstream, team-volume, participation/access-purchase, subscription, paid-retention, or headcount-driven Agency, eligibility, indirect, override, or residual economics; this does not bar the lawful provider’s own accepted-delivery proceeds under the separately approved direct-service rule below;
- no credit-score, deletion, dispute, approval, or other credit-outcome economics;
- no XP, Arena, popularity, raw activity, or Growth Reputation conversion;
- membership alone does not determine the payee; the lawful beneficiary follows the actual contract, worker relationship, assignment/consent, tax treatment, and law;
- no participant payment, purchase, subscription, certification, or access fee may improve eligibility, allocation, seller access, or fee rate;
- a participant-to-participant purchase, if later allowed, must be a voluntary bona fide direct transaction under a separately approved contract and related-party policy; it may pay only the lawful provider for accepted delivery and create no eligibility, pool, reputation, rank, override, or residual effect;
- one underlying fact has one economic use by default;
- no participant-funded pool or earlier-participant return funded by later participants;
- policy and budget freeze before the observation window;
- fixed maximum liability, source provenance, deterministic explanation, human review, and appeal;
- no shadow-to-live conversion;
- no live economics until legal, tax, accounting, privacy, security, payment, worker, and state/jurisdiction gates pass.

## 3. Agency Builder — input options

### 3.1 Input comparison

| Input | Alignment | Gaming/liability risk | Recommended treatment | Launch posture |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Verified active operators | Confirms a real actor behind evidence | Becomes per-head recruiting if count/addition changes money | Actor’s own evidence admissibility only | **Use in shadow only this way** |
| Retained operators | May signal durable organization quality | Blocked exits, inactive seats, complaint suppression, denominator gaming, quasi-employment optics | Diagnostic/downside guard; fair-exit and cohort integrity facts | **Do not positively weight** |
| Organization health | Captures stable operations | Composite scores hide assumptions and can reflect size/revenue | Atomic eligibility facts: good standing, integrity, fair exit, evidence reliability | **Eligibility floor** |
| Mentoring | Strong Founder alignment | Reciprocity, related parties, fake delivery, double compensation | Accepted organization-level stewardship only; cap; exclude directly paid service fact | **Later quality category** |
| Education | Strong Founder alignment | Low-quality volume, plagiarism, attendance/view gaming | Accepted usefulness, correction, maintenance, accessibility; never volume | **Later quality category** |
| Onboarding | Supports operator success | Recruitment-adjacent completion volume and control risk | Accepted quality/remediation evidence, independently verified and capped | **Later quality category** |
| Contribution quality | Strongest ecosystem-value fit | Reviewer capture, category stuffing, duplicated content | Source-owned acceptance, provenance, diversity, cap, correction path | **First shadow category** |
| Growth Reputation | Useful private explanation | Creates a score economy and black-box money multiplier | Never amount/eligibility/case input; provenance and explanation only; source-owned integrity facts are reviewed independently | **Never economic** |
| Retained customers/subscription duration | Could correlate with GCL revenue | Paid-retention incentives, CROA/business-opportunity/consumer-pressure risk | Performance diagnostic only; not compensation | **Reject** |
| Customer credit outcomes | Superficially attractive | Compliance, causal, fairness, privacy, and manipulation risk | Prohibited | **Never** |

### 3.2 Recommended Agency input stack by maturity

```text
Shadow:
  active actor admissibility
  + common-control grouping
  + organization integrity/fair-exit floor
  + one accepted organization-level contribution category

First possible live pilot:
  same eligibility stack
  + one equal unit per qualified common-control group
  + prospectively budgeted, company-funded, maximum-liability-capped pool

Mature only after proof:
  base unit
  + up to three separately capped quality units
  where one organization’s total quality units ≤ that organization’s base unit
```

The mature model cannot be considered until at least three qualification-shadow cycles and later controlled live evidence. The model intentionally omits a single weighted health/reputation score.

Common/beneficial-control truth has no ratified canonical owner. Every use in this options paper is a required control concept, not a storage assignment. An ownership ADR must define authority, evidence, correction, retention, privacy, and authorization before persistence or qualification use.

“Shadow” has two separate meanings:

- **qualification shadow:** no monetary values/payable units; may be participant-visible only after separate approval;
- **internal financial simulation:** synthetic or access-restricted hypothetical values for Finance; never participant-visible and never promotable.

## 4. Agency Growth Distribution — funding and allocation options

### 4.1 Funding-source options

| Option | Predictability | Liability | Incentive quality | Verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| Fixed company budget | High for GCL | Bounded | Neutral if not tied to cohort growth | **Recommended for first possible live pilot** |
| Percentage of gross subscriptions | Variable | Can outrun margin | Encourages subscription/paid-retention interpretations | **Reject** |
| Percentage of net revenue | Better than gross | Still not causal; accounting complexity | Can be mistaken for profit share/residual | **Reject for launch** |
| Percentage of verified incremental nonparticipant end-customer contribution margin | Economically grounded if causal and consistently defined | Bounded by real value | Hard to establish counterfactual; volatile; customer-to-participant/common-control definitions require counsel | **Study only as company-level affordability ceiling after proof** |
| Participant fees/purchases | Superficially self-funding | Severe legal/trust risk | Recruiting/pay-to-play incentives | **Never** |
| Sponsored/partner pool | Contractually bounded | Concentration and claim risk | May distort program toward sponsor interests | **Future separate program only** |

Recommended eventual pool rule. “Company-funded” states the prospectively approved source and maximum liability; it does not imply escrow, segregation, full cash backing, or statutory safeguarding. Approved-obligation backing remains separately TBD:

```text
cycle maximum liability = min(
  Founder/Finance-approved fixed cycle budget,
  Finance-verified affordability ceiling from an approved
  participant-independent company source after declared costs
)
```

If a later causal model is approved, the second term may be bounded only by verified incremental margin from **nonparticipant end-customer transactions** after all included customer-level variable costs—or another counsel-approved participant-independent company source. Payments by any CGn participant, applicant, economically eligible person, or commonly controlled entity are excluded. Participant purchases and participant retention, individually and in aggregate, may never affect whether a pool opens, the pool size, qualification, or allocation. Counsel must define “nonparticipant end-customer,” conversion, and common-control treatment. The percentage cannot be responsibly selected without CreditVector’s actual margin, churn, service-cost, and counterfactual data.

### 4.2 Allocation-model options

| Option | Benefits | Failure mode | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fixed dollar per qualified organization | Easy to explain; predictable per participant | Open-ended company liability and shell-org incentive | **Defer / not first choice** |
| Equal units from fixed pool | Bounded; transparent; avoids false precision | Dilution as cohort grows; common-control gaming | **Recommended first possible live model** |
| Fixed percentage of pool per organization | Predictable only with tiny fixed cohort | Cannot sum safely as cohort changes; arbitrary | **Reject** |
| Tier percentages/ranks | Familiar and motivating | Cliffs, timing games, status hierarchy, MLM optics | **Never** |
| Pure weighted quality score | Flexible and apparently precise | Opaque tuning, appeal complexity, score economy | **Reject for launch** |
| Equal base plus capped quality units | Balances access and differentiated value | More review cost; category gaming | **Recommended mature model after proof** |
| Auction/competitive prize | Fixed budget | Popularity, scarcity theater, inconsistent recurring cadence | **Reject** |

### 4.3 Cap options

| Cap | Purpose | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Total cycle pool | Maximum GCL liability | Mandatory; fixed prospectively |
| Common-control group | Stops shell-organization multiplication | Mandatory; aggregate first |
| Participant concentration | Prevents a small number of groups consuming the pool | Mandatory prospective cap; value **TBD BY FOUNDER/FINANCE**; stress-test 10% internally |
| Quality category | Limits one metric’s incentive gradient | Mandatory for mature hybrid; each category capped |
| Total quality units | Prevents quality add-ons overwhelming equal participation | Total quality units no greater than base unit |
| Lifetime earnings | Reduces successful-participant upside and creates arbitrary cliffs | **Reject**; use cycle and concentration caps instead |
| Per-operator/per-mentorship amount inside Agency pool | Appears measurable | Reintroduces headcount or duplicates Path B compensation | **Never** |

The 10% concentration cap is a noncanonical simulation hypothesis, not a ratification recommendation or legal/anti-pyramid/fairness safe harbor. A live value requires verified cohort and risk data. Capped or undistributed remainder remains with GCL and is not redistributed in-cycle.

## 6. Professional Operator — income-path portfolio

“Launch” means eligible for a future controlled pilot only after all gates.

### 6.1 First direct-value research candidates

| Path | Buyer value | Proof of delivery | Main risks | Recommended commercial model |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B2B operator professional-development mentoring | Operator operations, compliance, accessibility, or platform-use session/program; no consumer-specific credit improvement, dispute execution, legal advice, bureau/creditor representation, advance-fee guidance, or outcome claim | Accepted session, scoped artifact, maturity/no-show rules | Worker/control, related-party fraud, credit-services scope, no-shows | Lawful payee share under actual contract; fee TBD from cost model |
| Instructor-led B2B operator education, training, or speaking | Live workshop/training/speaking engagement tied to an approved professional-development objective and the same credit-services exclusions | Accepted curriculum/delivery—not attendance as economic value | Content accuracy, refunds, IP, accessibility, credit-services scope | Same simple fee architecture; no engagement-minute allocation |

Why this sequence:

- a real buyer and beneficiary exist;
- membership alone does not determine the payee; the actual contract and worker relationship do;
- delivery and dispute facts are understandable;
- concierge operation can test demand before product infrastructure;
- fully loaded support and moderation cost can be measured;
- it avoids paying an agency for diffuse value before causality is established.

## 7. Professional Operator fee scenarios — noncanonical

This section is sensitivity analysis only. The Decision Matrix ratifies no rate, range, midpoint, fee base, price, or operator share. Final values are **TBD BY FOUNDER/FINANCE/COUNSEL FROM VERIFIED COST AND DEMAND EVIDENCE**.

### 7.1 Fee-base definition

Before comparing rates, define one base:

```text
gross transaction value
− indirect taxes collected from buyer
− refunds, credits, and chargebacks
= net sale value

net sale value
− separately allocated provider/payment cost (only if contract says so)
= net service proceeds fee base
```

The participant statement must disclose each line. “Operator share” and “platform fee” cannot be calculated against shifting or hidden denominators.

### 7.2 Fee-structure comparison

| Option | GCL economics | Participant clarity | Risks | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0% launch subsidy | Negative until later | Very clear | Attracts supply without proving willingness to support costs; later fee shock | **Avoid except explicit research subsidy** |
| 10% flat fee | Lean platform economics | Clear | May not cover concierge, moderation, disputes, tax/payment cost | **Sensitivity case only** |
| 15% flat fee | More room for support and trust operations | Clear | Still must prove fully loaded margin | **Sensitivity midpoint only** |
| 20% flat fee | Better cost coverage | Clear | May suppress supply or feel high for self-sourced demand | **Sensitivity case only** |
| Discovery-dependent fee | Monetizes platform demand generation | Complex | Attribution gaming, channel conflict, opaque effective take rate | **Defer** |
| Subscription plus lower fee | Potential predictable GCL revenue | Complex and paid-access risk | Makes paid access economically advantageous | **Never** |
| Seller tiers/volume discounts | Rewards scale | Harder to explain | Creates status, gaming, and small-seller disadvantage | **Avoid** |
| Buyer service fee | Preserves operator share | Buyer sees added cost | Checkout abandonment and fee disclosure risk | **Defer until price research** |

Scenario instruction: model **10%–20%** with **15% only as the sensitivity midpoint**, then discard or replace the range when concierge research establishes actual demand and cost. Do not ask the Founder to ratify the range or advertise an operator share before the rate, base, fee responsibility, taxes, refunds, and examples are separately approved.

### 7.3 Market context, not a pricing answer

Official platform pages reviewed on 2026-07-31 show that creator fees vary widely with discovery, merchant-of-record responsibility, and support:

- [Gumroad pricing](https://gumroad.com/pricing) lists its current fee and merchant-of-record posture. Its separate [prohibited-products page](https://gumroad.com/prohibited) is a risk signal because it excludes credit-repair/protection services.
- [Patreon](https://support.patreon.com/hc/en-us/articles/11111747095181-Creator-fees-overview) describes a standard platform percentage plus processing for newer creator pages.
- [Maven](https://help.maven.com/en/articles/12240853-maven-expert-policy-guidebook) describes its expert/platform split and separate processing treatment.
- [Udemy](https://support.udemy.com/hc/en-us/articles/229605008-Instructor-revenue-share) uses materially different shares for instructor-led versus platform-originated sales.

These examples justify sensitivity testing; they do not establish CreditVector’s lawful role, costs, or optimal fee.

### 8.1 Cadence

| Option | Advantages | Costs | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly on the 15th | Predictable; Founder-ratified; clean accounting cadence | Creates salary/residual expectation and operating deadline | **Agency Distribution only** |

```text

days 13–14: maker-checker approval and statement
day 15: scheduled instruction only when all payable conditions, including the later-ratified threshold, are satisfied
non-banking day: next banking business day
```

Never promise receipt on the 15th.

## 9. Reversal, dispute, and suspension options

### 9.1 Reversal

Use append-only state and accounting history:

```text
source invalidation / fraud / refund / accounting error
  → case and evidence
  → human decision
  → notice
  → compensating entry
  → appeal
  → same-stream recovery only if agreement and law permit
```

Never delete the original record, silently change a statement, automatically debit a bank account, or offset unrelated earnings.

### 9.2 Suspension

| Model | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Automated global account shutdown from a fraud score | **Never** |
| Scope-specific prospective suspension by a named human authority | **Recommended** |
| Hold all streams because one item is disputed | **Reject unless legally required and factually linked** |
| Indefinite hold without review date | **Never** |
| Emergency stop with scope, reason, review time, and later ordinary governance | **Recommended** |

### 9.3 Appeal

Minimum control categories; all service targets and contractual windows remain TBD:

- notice identifying decision, policy version, reason code, material source facts, and effect;
- participant access to relevant evidence subject to privacy/security redaction;
- a counsel-approved dispute/service target that never waives longer statutory or contractual rights;
- independent reviewer with conflict disclosure;
- an Operations/counsel-approved substantive-decision target or written extension;
- correction at the authoritative source when the source fact is wrong;
- no retaliation or Reputation/XP penalty for using the appeal process.

No time value is recommended for ratification before operating-capacity and counsel review.

## 11. Unit-economics decision model

### 11.1 Agency Distribution

Treat Agency Distribution as a quality investment until causality is proven. Use the same declared accounting base as Decision 21:

```text
Agency stream contribution
  = verified incremental nonparticipant end-customer revenue versus matched baseline,
    or other counsel-approved participant-independent company value
  − customer-level variable costs included in the declared base
  − Agency distributions and Agency-only review, appeal, integrity, support,
    tax, accounting, payment, privacy, security, legal, reserve,
    discovery/CAC, and variable infrastructure/AI costs not already deducted
```

Any verified avoided cost is reflected only by reducing the corresponding named cost term; it is never added again as a second value term. Non-monetized ecosystem value remains a product outcome, not an additive dollar amount. Go only when Agency stream contribution is positive at a Founder-approved confidence level and horizon, with no material correlation to prohibited inputs.

### 11.2 Professional Operator

```text
Professional stream contribution
  = platform fee revenue
  − refunds, chargebacks, and fraud loss
  − provider/payment cost borne by GCL
  − Professional-stream-direct support, moderation, integrity, tax, accounting,
    privacy, security, legal, reserve, discovery/CAC,
    and variable infrastructure/AI costs not already deducted
```

Measure at median and tail transaction size; a positive average can hide loss-making low-value transactions. Each deduction appears once.

### 11.3 Consolidated portfolio contribution

```text
portfolio contribution
  = sum of Agency and Professional stream contributions
  − shared incremental costs not allocated to or deducted in any stream,
    counted once
  with inter-stream transfers eliminated
```

### 11.4 Required pilot measures

- buyer repeat-value rate and accepted-delivery rate;
- refund, chargeback, no-show, dispute, reversal, and complaint rate;
- manual review, support, moderation, accessibility, and correction minutes per transaction/cycle;
- common-control and related-party rate;
- concentration by seller, buyer, organization, and stream;
- participant net proceeds after platform/provider fees and typical documented expenses;
- GCL fully loaded contribution margin;
- evidence replay and false-positive/appeal overturn rate;
- correlation with operator additions, headcount, purchases, paid retention, activity, popularity, and credit outcomes;
- differential outcomes by organization size, tenure, accessibility needs, and other lawful fairness cohorts.

Founder/Finance/Product must set the numeric go/kill thresholds from real baselines. The Growth architecture cannot supply them honestly.

## 12. Launch strategy options

| Strategy | Speed | Learning quality | Risk | Decision |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Build full platform then launch | Slow | Low before demand | Very high sunk cost | **Reject** |
| National multi-stream launch | Fast surface breadth | Confounded | Critical legal/operational risk | **Never as first launch** |
| Paid Agency pilot first | Tests Founder thesis directly | Low causal clarity | Creates income expectation before value proof | **Reject** |
| Unpaid Agency qualification shadow + B2B operator-development demand research | Moderate | Highest separation of hypotheses | Requires manual operations and specialist boundary | **Recommended** |
| Professional service only, abandon Agency now | Simplest economics | Does not test organization thesis | May give up differentiator too early | **Fallback if shadow fails** |

Recommended sequence:

1. Founder ratifies decisions; no implementation.
2. If separately authorized, Phase 1A design packet for one unpaid Agency shadow wedge.
3. Specialist matrices and one-jurisdiction decision.
4. Only later, dormant non-monetary shadow engineering if separately authorized.
5. Concierge demand research for B2B operator professional-development mentoring without building marketplace infrastructure, only under counsel-approved operations and the stated credit-services exclusions.
6. If direct value, CROA/state credit-services boundaries, worker/payee treatment, and operating controls pass, separately consider one controlled live pilot.
7. If Agency shadow proves distinct causal value, separately authorize fixed-pool design; money remains later than Professional service.
8. Expand education, then courses/templates/Marketplace/Kai Assets only after repeat demand.
9. Consider Growth Partner/affiliate last.
