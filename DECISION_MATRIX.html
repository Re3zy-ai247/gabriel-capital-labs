Mission Control, Agency Command, Arena, Community, and the CXOS room implementation are outside this document's change scope. This CGn stream does not modify them; the shared worktree contains concurrent changes owned by other streams.

Non-negotiable economic laws#

The following are VERIFIED Founder principles and are not reopened by this matrix:

  1. CreditVector does not reward recruiting; it rewards building successful organizations.
  2. Contribution matters more than popularity.
  3. Retention matters more than acquisition.
  4. Education matters more than marketing.
  5. Mentorship matters more than referrals.
  6. Verified evidence matters more than claimed success.
  7. Growth must strengthen the ecosystem, not extract from it.

The following constructs are therefore PROHIBITED:

  • compensation for invitations, signups, recruiting, headcount, downline depth, or participation/access/subscription purchases; this does not prohibit the Decision 14 lawful provider’s own accepted-delivery proceeds under a separately approved direct transaction;
  • unlocks, multipliers, ranks, tiers, or residuals based on recruiting or retained subscriptions;
  • self-referral compensation;
  • compensation derived from downstream participant activity;
  • pay-to-qualify certifications, inventory loading, or required purchases;
  • conversion of XP, reputation, popularity, challenges, badges, or engagement streaks into money; and
  • claims or interfaces implying guaranteed, passive, typical, or salary-like income.

Decision 01 — Initial economic wedge#

Proposal: Begin with a fixed, invited Agency ecosystem-stewardship qualification-shadow cohort while separately researching B2B operator professional-development mentoring and instructor-led B2B operator education/training/speaking as the first direct-value Professional Operator candidates.

The Agency shadow cohort must evaluate organization-level value that is independent of recruiting, member purchases, or headcount. It must display qualification reasons only—no dollar amounts, wallet balance, accrued value, or payment promise.

Rationale: Agency economics require proof that CreditVector receives distinct, measurable organization-level value. B2B operator development has a clearer provider, beneficiary, service, and consideration relationship, but remains blocked until counsel defines a curriculum excluding consumer-specific credit improvement, dispute execution, legal advice, bureau/creditor representation, advance-fee guidance, and outcome claims.

Kill rule: If CreditVector cannot identify and verify organization-level consideration distinct from member services, subscriptions, and recruiting, the Agency Growth Distribution must be abandoned. Professional Operators may still be paid directly for separately purchased and accepted services.

Decision 07 — Pool, liability, and concentration controls#

Proposal: Before any future qualification window opens, the responsible finance approver must freeze the maximum company-funded liability for that cycle. Accrued obligations may never exceed that amount.

“Company-funded” means GCL prospectively approves the participant-independent funding source and maximum liability. It does not imply escrow, segregation, 100% cash backing, or statutory safeguarding. The amount and instrument backing approved obligations remain governed by Decision 08.

Required controls:

  • one economic unit and one cap per verified beneficial-owner/common-control group;
  • an organization-level cap independent of operator count;
  • a prospectively fixed concentration cap for one common-control group, with the numeric value determined from verified cohort and risk data;
  • cohort admission frozen before the window;
  • no retroactive increase in the denominator, weights, or eligible cohort;
  • no borrowing from a later cycle;
  • no funding from another participant's withheld or reversed amount; and
  • a separate, Finance/Accounting-approved operating contingency that is not represented as participant money; its amount and instrument remain TBD.

No numeric concentration cap, dollar pool budget, or contingency value is ratified here. Finance may stress-test a 10% cap in internal financial simulation, but it is not a recommendation or legal/anti-pyramid/fairness safe harbor. Finance, accounting, counsel, and the Founder must approve final values in a versioned schedule before a live pilot. Capped or undistributed remainder remains with GCL and is not redistributed in-cycle.

Recommendation: RATIFY CONTROL DESIGN; ALL NUMERIC VALUES REMAIN TBD FROM VERIFIED MODELS.

Decision 08 — Reserve and holdback policy#

Proposal: Distinguish every reserve/hold concept and assign its owner before live use:

  • Company funding allocation: GCL liquidity prospectively assigned to a program/cycle; not participant property.
  • Approved-obligation backing: accountant-defined backing for approved unpaid obligations; value/instrument TBD. A 100% cash scenario is internal stress testing, not proof of solvency.
  • Accounting allowance: accountant-owned estimate for expected loss/refund/error treatment.
  • Provider reserve: provider-specific settlement control, separately disclosed/reconciled.
  • Statutory safeguarding assets: separate legal protection only if payments counsel identifies a duty.
  • Program contingency: Finance/Accounting-owned uncertainty budget with amount and risk horizon TBD.
  • Case hold: freeze on a specific unpaid item based on documented risk evidence.

Do not use a blanket participant holdback for an approved Agency obligation. Mature evidence before approval. A case hold may apply only to a specific unpaid item under documented risk and must have a maximum duration or review date, release rule, notice, evidence basis, appeal route, and human owner. Direct-service and marketplace proceeds require separate refund/chargeback reserve policies and cannot borrow the Agency rule.

Decision 09 — Suspension, reversals, and disputes#

Proposal: Qualification may be suspended for unresolved identity, source-evidence, sanctions, fraud, legal, or tax defects. Suspension must be reason-coded, scoped, reviewable, and time-bounded where law permits.

A posted obligation may be reversed only for:

  • invalidated source evidence;
  • proven fraud or material policy evasion;
  • duplicate payment or calculation error; or
  • a legally required correction.

Every reversal must be a compensating ledger entry preserving the original record. It requires notice, rationale, evidence references, approver identity, and an appeal path. CreditVector may not silently edit history, automatically debit a participant's bank account, or voluntarily net/recover against an unrelated stream. Any legally compelled collection is handled outside stream ledgers under separate authority.

Unresolved evidence is held, not estimated. A successful post-cycle appeal is resolved through a later compensating entry, never by rewriting the closed cycle.

Recommendation: RATIFY.

Decision 12 — Separation of economic instruments#

Proposal: Keep the following concepts logically, contractually, and eventually technically distinct:

  1. evidence event;
  2. qualification decision;
  3. reputation projection;
  4. attribution;
  5. benefit or entitlement;
  6. economic obligation;

Decision 17 — Growth Reputation#

Decision 21 — Unit economics and success standard#

Proposal: Approve a future live path only when its defined, fully loaded, counterfactual unit economics are positive without counting revenue or cost twice:

Agency stream contribution
  = verified incremental nonparticipant end-customer revenue versus matched baseline,
    or other counsel-approved participant-independent company value
  − customer-level variable costs included in the declared base
  − Agency distributions and Agency-only review, appeal, integrity, support,
    tax, accounting, payment, privacy, security, legal, reserve,
    discovery/CAC, and variable infrastructure/AI costs not already deducted

Professional stream contribution
  = platform fee revenue
  − refunds, chargebacks, provider, support, moderation, integrity,
    tax, accounting, privacy, security, legal, reserve,
    discovery/CAC, and variable infrastructure/AI costs not already deducted

Portfolio contribution
  = sum of stream contributions
  − shared incremental costs not allocated to or deducted in any stream,
    counted once
  with inter-stream transfers eliminated

Every base must state which deductions it already includes. “Incremental” requires evidence that value would not have occurred without the program. Gross transaction volume, participant count, content volume, and attributed signups are not proof of durable value.

Primary operating measures should include:

  • verified repeat value rate;
  • time to first verified value;
  • repeat buyers or repeat accepted outcomes;
  • retained gross profit after fully loaded costs;
  • support, review, and moderation cost per accepted outcome;
  • refund, reversal, dispute, and successful-appeal rates;
  • concentration and common-control exposure;
  • fraud false-positive and false-negative findings;
  • fixed-pool and reserve utilization; and
  • expectation-related complaints and support contacts.

Decision 22 — Automatic pause and kill criteria#

Proposal: A shadow or future pilot must pause when any of these occurs:

  • unreconciled ledger or evidence totals;
  • liability or reserve limit exceeded or at credible risk of exceedance;
  • a material privacy, security, access-control, or data-lineage incident;
  • inability to explain or reproduce a material decision;
  • material complaint, dispute, reversal, or successful-appeal rate above its predeclared limit;
  • concentration above its predeclared limit without independently verified value;
  • evidence that Agency, eligibility, indirect, override, or residual earning depends on recruiting, headcount, participation/access/subscription purchases, or paid retention; the Decision 14 provider’s own accepted-delivery proceeds alone are not this trigger;
  • adverse legal, compliance, tax, accounting, worker-classification, or payment-custody finding; or
  • fully loaded economics below the predeclared viability threshold.

A path must be killed, not merely tuned, when:

  • Agency or indirect value cannot be causally separated from recruiting or participant payments, or Professional proceeds cannot be causally limited to the lawful provider’s own bona fide accepted delivery;
  • a participant cannot reasonably succeed without building an audience or recruiting;
  • the business model requires continuing participant growth to meet prior obligations;
  • compliant explanation would materially undermine conversion economics;
  • fraud controls require disproportionate surveillance or participant harm; or
  • repeated shadow cycles cannot produce reliable, contestable evidence at an acceptable operating cost.

Pause and kill thresholds must be frozen before a pilot begins. No threshold is numerically approved here.

Decision 23 — Participant expectation policy#
